03 Theorem — Perpendicular from Centre to Chord

Proposition 7

Given: AB is a chord other than a diameter; O is centre; OD ⟂ AB.

To Prove: AD = DB.

1
Join OA and OB.
2
OA = OB because both are radii.
3
OD is common and ∠ODA = ∠ODB = 90°.
4
ΔOAD ≅ ΔOBD by RHS.
5
Corresponding sides give AD = DB.

Verification: Equal radii + common side + right angles establish RHS congruence.

Converse — Theorem 2.1

Given: AB is a chord, D is midpoint, so AD=DB; O is centre.

To Prove: OD ⟂ AB.

1
Join OA and OB.
2
OA=OB (radii), AD=DB (midpoint), OD common.
3
ΔOAD ≅ ΔOBD by SSS.
4
Therefore ∠ADO=∠BDO.
5
These adjacent angles form 180°, so each is 90°.
∴ OD ⟂ AB

04 Equal Chords and Distance from Centre

THEOREM 8
Equal chords ⇒ Equidistant from centre

Given AB=CD and OE, OF perpendicular to the chords, prove OE=OF.

1
Perpendicular from centre bisects chords: AE=EB and CF=FD.
2
AE=½AB and CF=½CD.
3
AB=CD ⇒ AE=CF.
4
OA=OC (radii) and right angles are equal.
5
ΔOEA ≅ ΔOFC by RHS ⇒ OE=OF.
CONVERSE — THEOREM 2.3
Equidistant chords ⇒ Equal chords

Source proof uses right-triangle congruence and chord-bisecting property.

Source also states: a chord farther from the centre is smaller; a smaller chord is farther from the centre. fileciteturn27file7L286-L301

05 One Circle Through Three Non-collinear Points

THEOREM 2.2
There is one and only one circle through three non-collinear points.

Construction: Draw perpendicular bisectors of AB and BC. Their intersection is O. Since OA=OB=OC, the circle with centre O and radius OA passes through A, B and C.

06 Central Angle & Equal Chords

∠
Chord AB ଦ୍ୱାରା centre O ରେ ତିଆରି ∠AOB କୁ central angle କୁହାଯାଏ।
PROPOSITION 9
Equal chords ⇒ Equal central angles

Given: AB=CD. To Prove: ∠AOB=∠COD.

1
Join OA, OB, OC, OD.
2
OA=OC and OB=OD (radii); AB=CD (given).
3
ΔOAB ≅ ΔOCD by SSS.
4
Therefore ∠AOB=∠COD.
The converse is also stated: chords subtending congruent central angles are congruent. fileciteturn27file0L27-L30

07 Arc — Minor, Major & Semicircle

Minor Arc

Smaller arc between two endpoints; degree measure below 180°.

Major Arc

The larger/opposite arc; degree measure above 180°.

Semicircle

An arc whose corresponding chord is a diameter.

Two arcs between A and B; if AB is diameter, each is a semicircle.

08 Inscribed Angle — Theorem 2.6

CORE FORMULA
Inscribed angle = ½ × degree measure of opposite arc

ଏଠାରେ “opposite arc” ଠିକ୍ identify କରିବା ସବୁଠାରୁ important step.

Opposite arc = θ°
→
Inscribed angle
→
θ/2°

Important consequences

1
Same segment ର all inscribed angles are equal.
2
Opposite/supplementary arc ର corresponding inscribed angles are equal.
3
Angle in a semicircle = 90°.
4
If an inscribed angle is 90°, its intercepted arc is a semicircle.

10 Cyclic Quadrilateral

▱
ଯଦି quadrilateral ର ସମସ୍ତ four vertices ଗୋଟିଏ circle ଉପରେ ଥାଏ, ତେବେ ଏହା cyclic quadrilateral.
THEOREM 2.11
∠A + ∠C = 180°
∠B + ∠D = 180°

Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral are supplementary.

Proof structure

Given: ABCD cyclic. To Prove: opposite angles supplementary.

1
Opposite angles stand on two arcs whose total degree measure is 360°.
2
By Theorem 2.6 each inscribed angle is half its corresponding arc.
3
Therefore ∠A+∠C = ½(360°) = 180°.
4
Similarly ∠B+∠D = 180°.

Verification: Both opposite pairs add to 180°.

CONVERSE — THEOREM 2.8
If opposite angles are supplementary, the quadrilateral is cyclic.
Source pp. 62–64 gives the definition and theorem. fileciteturn28file4L149-L172 fileciteturn30file1L147-L171

11 Intersecting Chords & Secants

INTERSECTING CHORDS
PA × PB = PC × PD

Given: chords AB and CD intersect at interior P. To Prove: PA·PB=PC·PD.

1
Join CA and BD.
2
Use equal/same-segment inscribed angles and vertically opposite angles.
3
ΔPAC ~ ΔPBD by AA.
4
AP/PD = PC/PB.
5
Cross-multiply: AP·PB = PC·PD.

Verification: The product equality follows from the similarity ratio.

EXTERNAL SECANTS
PA × PB = PC × PD

Source Example 2 proves the same product relation for two secants from an exterior point.

ANGLE BETWEEN TWO EXTERNAL SECANTS
m∠APC = ½ [m⌢BD − m⌢AC]

12 Solved Numerical Examples

Example 1 — Chord 16 cm, radius 10 cm

Given: AB=16 cm, r=10 cm, D is midpoint. To Find: DP.

Method: Perpendicular from centre bisects chord.

AD = DB = 16/2 = 8 cm
10² = 8² + DP²
100 = 64 + DP²
DP² = 36
DP = 6 cm

Answer: DP=6 cm.

Verification: 8²+6²=64+36=100=10².

Example 2 — Equal parallel chords

Given: AB=CD=8 cm, r=5 cm, AB ∥ CD. To Find: distance between chords.

Half chord = 8/2 = 4 cm
d = √(r² − (AB/2)²)
d = √(5² − 4²)
d = √(25−16)=3 cm
Total distance = 3+3 = 6 cm

Answer: 6 cm.

Verification: Equal chords are equidistant from centre.

Example 3 — Arc 80°

Given: Opposite arc=80°. To Find: inscribed angle.

Angle = ½ × 80° = 40°

Answer: 40°.

Verification: 2×40°=80°.

Example 4 — Cyclic quadrilateral

Given: ∠A=50°, ∠B=120°. To Find: ∠C, ∠D.

∠A+∠C=180°
50°+∠C=180° ⇒ ∠C=130°
∠B+∠D=180°
120°+∠D=180° ⇒ ∠D=60°

Answer: ∠C=130°, ∠D=60°.

Verification: 50+130=180 and 120+60=180.
