A. LEAD-IN

Why do we admire Kapil Dev?

Cricket is an immensely popular game in India.
India won the previous Prudential World Cup in 1983 under Kapil Dev's captaincy.
He became a household name in India and wherever cricket is played.
He is known as one of the best all-rounders in the world.
The lesson also asks us to learn about his admirable qualities as a human being.
⭐ Exam Point: The lesson presents both Kapil's cricketing achievement and his human qualities.
B. THE TEXT • 1978

Gavaskar notices a young Kapil

1978: Raj Singh Dungarpur called Gavaskar to discuss India's next cricket moves. Gavaskar mentioned a young, impressive fast bowler named Kapil Dev who showed enormous potential and was willing to learn.
Key Point: Gavaskar saw Kapil's willingness to receive coaching and his eagerness to improve—signs of a champion in the making.
QUICK LEARNER

Gamesmanship? No — genuine advice

Some players thought Gavaskar was using gamesmanship to make Kapil bowl the wrong line.
That was farthest from Gavaskar's mind; he wanted to play against good, fast bowlers.
Kapil acted on the advice in the next over and came closer to the stumps.
Once he got it right, Gavaskar saw that Kapil was going to be a force in cricket.
📝 Gamesmanship: In this context, a competitive tactic/trick intended to gain an advantage.
EAST AFRICA TOUR

Experience of foreign conditions

Kapil bowled extremely well, got vital breakthroughs and helped his side win.
The team included experienced Test players and promising youngsters.
Test stalwarts: Pataudi, Vishwanath, Yajurvinder Singh and Eknath Solkar.
Youngsters: Kapil Dev and Suru Nayak.
Playing abroad against different opposition, conditions, bowlers and players helps make a better cricketer.
Kapil hit many towering sixes and won East African cricket lovers' hearts.
He improved match by match and was wellnigh unplayable against the strongest East African side.
🎯 Answer: The youngsters selected were Kapil Dev and Suru Nayak.

THE ALL-ROUNDER

Batting + Bowling

59 as night-watchman → All-rounder tag → Century vs West Indies
Kapil scored 59 as a night-watchman and earned the all-rounder tag.
He later scored a century against the West Indians.
On the 1979 tour of England, he failed with the bat but bowled with customary fire and efficiency.
He got out in eagerness to hit the ball in the air instead of taking his time.
This was due to inexperience; on the 1982 England tour he scored 300 runs in three Tests.

EXPERIENCE

From mistakes to maturity

Greater experience and responsibility after becoming captain made loose shots rare.
The author thinks India could have won the 1979 Oval Test, drawn after falling nine runs short.
Kapil was promoted in the batting order but hit the first delivery and was caught at long on.
The mature Kapil, the author says, would have taken singles and twos, watched the bowling and then played his shots.
"There is no place for ifs and buts in cricket."
1983 PRUDENTIAL WORLD CUP

Kapil's Greatest Triumph

June 1983: India won the Prudential World Cup under Kapil's captaincy.
Zimbabwe Match: Kapil took 5 wickets for 17 runs and scored 175 not out—the turning point of the tournament.
IMPACT

Victory inspired youngsters

Kapil's grinning face holding the Prudential Trophy became as memorable as the win.
His brothers started Hotel Kapil as a tribute.
His success gave encouragement and impetus to thousands of youngsters across the country.
His advent was described as a major positive development because India had not previously had a fast bowler who lifted national prestige so high.
His example inspired boys to take up fast bowling and helped Indian batsmen get practice against pace.
His attacking brand made him a crowd puller and encouraged attractive cricket.

KAPIL AS A PERSON

Success did not change his attitude

He rose from a lad batting at No.11 in East Africa to a leading all-rounder.
He remained the same simple fellow he was in 1978.
Success brought greater confidence in dealing with people.
He remained polite and courteous to senior cricketers.
He was prepared to listen to everyone.
⭐ Human qualities: simple • confident • polite • courteous • willing to listen.
C. ABOUT THE AUTHOR

Sunil Manohar Gavaskar

Born: 10 July 1949.
Former Indian cricketer who played for India in the 1970s and 1980s.
Regarded as one of the greatest opening batsmen in cricket history.
Held the record of 34 Test centuries for almost two decades before Sachin Tendulkar broke it in December 2005.
Received the Arjuna Award (1975) and Padma Bhusan (1980).
📌 The piece was written in 1983 and focuses on Kapil's early days and his performance as player and captain in the 1983 Prudential World Cup.
