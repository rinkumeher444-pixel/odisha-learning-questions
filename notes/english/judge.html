ODISHA CLASS 10 • ENGLISH POETRY

VILLAGE SONG

Sarojini Naidu

Mother↔Daughter→Forest→Nature & Fairy Folk
🎯 Learning Objective: ବୁଝିବା ଯେ କବିତାରେ ମାଆ ଝିଅକୁ ବିବାହ ଦିଗକୁ ରଖିବାକୁ ଚାହୁଁଛନ୍ତି, କିନ୍ତୁ ଝିଅଟି ଜଙ୍ଗଲର ପ୍ରକୃତି ଓ ଶିଶୁସୁଲଭ ସ୍ୱାଧୀନତା ଦିଗକୁ ଯିବାକୁ ଚାହୁଁଛି।
A. LEAD-IN

କବିତାର ପୃଷ୍ଠଭୂମି

Marriage is presented as an important event in a person’s life.
Parents generally want their children to be happily married and settled.
ଏହି କବିତାରେ ଏକ young girl ବିବାହ କରିବାକୁ ଚାହୁଁନାହିଁ।
🎯 ମୁଖ୍ୟ ପ୍ରଶ୍ନ: Why does the girl take this decision?
B. LET’S LISTEN

ଶୁଣିବା ଓ ଅନୁଭବ କରିବା

Listen→Notice voice & tone→Follow the poem→Read silently→Use notes & glossary
📌 Teacher reads the poem aloud. Students notice the teacher’s voice, tone and expressions, then follow the reading and identify words, phrases and expressions that appeal to them.
C. THE TEXT

କବିତା — Stanza by Stanza

Stanza 1 — Mother’s questions

Honey child, honey child, whither are you going?
Would you cast your jewels all to the breezes blowing?
Would you leave the mother who on golden grain has fed you?
Would you grieve the lover who is riding forth to wed you?
Simple English: The stanza shows mother’s questions.
ସହଜ ଓଡ଼ିଆ ବ୍ୟାଖ୍ୟା:
ମାଆ ଝିଅକୁ ସ୍ନେହରେ “honey child” ବୋଲି ଡାକି ପଚାରୁଛନ୍ତି—ସେ କେଉଁଠି ଯାଉଛି? ନିଜର ଗହଣା ଛାଡ଼ିଦେବ କି? ଯେ ମାଆ ତାକୁ ଖାଇବାକୁ ଦେଇ ପାଳିଛନ୍ତି, ତାଙ୍କୁ ଛାଡ଼ିଯିବ କି? ବିବାହ ପାଇଁ ଆସୁଥିବା loverକୁ ଦୁଃଖ ଦେବ କି?

Stanza 2 — Daughter’s answer

Mother mine, to the wild forest I am going,
Where upon the champa boughs the champa buds are blowing;
To the koil-haunted river-isles where lotus lilies glisten,
The voices of the fairy folk are calling me: O listen!
Simple English: The stanza shows daughter’s answer.
ସହଜ ଓଡ଼ିଆ ବ୍ୟାଖ୍ୟା:
ଝିଅଟି କହୁଛି—ସେ wild forestକୁ ଯିବ। ସେଠାରେ champa ଗଛର ଡାଳରେ champa କଢ଼ି ଫୁଟୁଛି, koilର ସ୍ୱର ଥିବା river-islesରେ lotus lilies ଚମକୁଛି, ଏବଂ fairy folkର ସ୍ୱର ତାକୁ ଡାକୁଛି।

Stanza 3 — Mother describes worldly pleasure

Honey child, honey child, the world is full of pleasure,
Of bridal-songs and cradle-songs and sandal-scented leisure.
Your bridal robes are in the loom, silver and saffron glowing,
Your bridal cakes are on the hearth: O whither are you going?
Simple English: The stanza shows mother describes worldly pleasure.
ସହଜ ଓଡ଼ିଆ ବ୍ୟାଖ୍ୟା:
ମାଆ କହୁଛନ୍ତି ଯେ ଜଗତ ଆନନ୍ଦରେ ପୂର୍ଣ୍ଣ। ବିବାହ ଗୀତ, ଶିଶୁକୁ ଶୁଆଇବାର cradle-songs, sandalର ସୁଗନ୍ଧ, ବିବାହ ପୋଷାକ ଓ ଘରେ ପ୍ରସ୍ତୁତ bridal cakes—ଏସବୁ ତାଙ୍କ ଝିଅ ପାଇଁ ଅପେକ୍ଷା କରୁଛି।

Stanza 4 — Daughter rejects worldly pleasures

The bridal-songs and cradle-songs have cadences of sorrow,
The laughter of the sun to-day, the wind of death to-morrow.
Far sweeter sound the forest-notes where forest-streams are falling;
O mother mine, I cannot stay, the fairy-folk are calling.
Simple English: The stanza shows daughter rejects worldly pleasures.
ସହଜ ଓଡ଼ିଆ ବ୍ୟାଖ୍ୟା:
ଝିଅଟି କହୁଛି ଯେ bridal-songs ଓ cradle-songsରେ ମଧ୍ୟ sorrowର rhythm ରହିଛି। ଆଜିର happiness ଆସନ୍ତାକାଲିର sorrow/deathରେ ପରିଣତ ହୋଇପାରେ। ତାହାଠାରୁ forest streamsର ସ୍ୱର ତାକୁ ଅଧିକ ମିଠା ଲାଗୁଛି। Fairy folk ତାକୁ ଡାକୁଥିବାରୁ ସେ ରହିପାରିବ ନାହିଁ।

⭐ Exam Point: The poem contrasts worldly pleasures associated with marriage with the daughter’s attraction to nature and the fairy folk.
D. ABOUT THE POET

Sarojini Naidu

1879–1949
Born in Hyderabad on February 13, 1879.
A child prodigy, freedom fighter and poet.
She gave up her literary career to join the freedom struggle led by Mahatma Gandhi.
First Indian woman to become President of the Indian National Congress.
First woman to become Governor of Uttar Pradesh.
Popularly known as “The Nightingale of India”.
“Village Song” appeared in The Golden Threshold, first published by Dodo Press, London, 1905.
She passed away in 1949.
E. ABOUT THE POEM

Folk Poetry — ସରଳତା ଓ ସାମୂହିକ ଜୀବନ

Oral tradition→Customs & beliefs→Simple joys & sorrows→Collective life
Indian folk poetry has largely been oral.
It deals with customs, beliefs, traditions and superstitions.
It presents simple joys and sorrows, particularly of rural people.
Its language, vocabulary and imagery are drawn from everyday scenes and sights.
It was nurtured by wandering minstrels and was usually recitative.
The poem presents the voice of a mother and the voice of a daughter who wishes to remain a child in the forest.

G. LET’S UNDERSTAND

ବୁଝିବା ପାଇଁ ପ୍ରଶ୍ନ

H. LET’S APPRECIATE

କବିତାର ଗଭୀର ଅର୍ଥ

1. The first four lines pose four questions. The mother wants to tell her daughter not to leave her, her home and the marriage awaiting her.
2. The mother’s appeal does not stop the girl. The fairy folk and the sounds/beauty of the forest are irresistible to her.
3. Worldly pleasures are presented through bridal and cradle songs, bridal robes and cakes; the daughter finds the pleasures and sounds of nature sweeter.
I. LET’S DO SOME ACTIVITIES

Activity 1–6

1. How many times does “would” occur?
It occurs twice in the poem.
2. How many questions are asked? Who asks them?
The questions are asked by the mother. In the first stanza, there are four questions; the poem also contains the mother’s question “O whither are you going?” in the third stanza.
3. Use “bridal” in another expression.
Example: bridal necklace.
4. Use “who” to qualify nouns in your own sentences.
Example: The girl who loves nature went to the forest.
Example: The mother who cares for her child speaks gently.
5. Arrange the phrases in sequence.
koil-haunted river-isles → the champa buds are blowing → lotus lilies glisten → bridal robes are in the loom → bridal cakes are on the hearth
6. Rhyming words.
Examples from the stanzas include going / blowing / you / you, pleasure / leisure / glowing / going, and sorrow / tomorrow / falling / calling. Words ending in -ing recur and create a flowing sound while reading.
📌 Remember: Activity 6 asks students to mark rhyming words in each stanza and discuss the effect, especially the recurring “-ing” endings.

EXAM REVISION

⭐ 15 Key Points

1. “Village Song” is a poem by Sarojini Naidu.
2. The poem is presented as a conversation between a mother and daughter.
3. The mother calls the girl “honey child” affectionately.
4. The mother asks why the girl is leaving.
5. The girl says she is going to the wild forest.
6. The forest has champa buds, koil-haunted river-isles and glistening lotus lilies.
7. The fairy folk are calling the girl.
8. The mother describes worldly pleasures connected with marriage and family life.
9. Bridal songs and cradle songs are mentioned.
10. Bridal robes and bridal cakes are ready.
11. The girl says bridal and cradle songs have cadences of sorrow.
12. She contrasts today’s happiness with tomorrow’s wind of death.
13. Forest-notes sound sweeter to her.
14. The poem presents qualities of traditional folk poetry: simplicity, everyday imagery and collective life.
15. The daughter cannot stay because the fairy folk are calling.
